Soil Health Basics for Small Farms

Healthy soil grows healthy crops. Here is how to read, feed and protect the ground you farm.

Soil is the single most valuable asset on a small farm. It holds water, feeds roots and stores the nutrients your crops depend on. Yet it is easy to exhaust: continuous cropping, bare fallows and heavy chemical use all burn through organic matter.

Start with observation. Healthy soil crumbles in the hand, smells earthy, holds moisture without waterlogging and is full of visible life — earthworms, fine roots, fungal threads. If your soil sets hard after rain or runs to silt, it needs organic matter before it needs anything else.

Feed the soil, not just the plant. Farmyard compost, well-rotted manure, green manure crops and crop residues all rebuild organic matter. Apply compost before the planting season and incorporate it lightly; deep inversion buries the biology you are trying to build.

Keep the ground covered. Bare soil loses moisture fast, heats up and erodes in the first heavy rain. Residue mulch, cover crops and contour planting are cheap, low-risk ways to hold soil and moisture through the dry months.

Rotate and rest. Cereals followed by legumes break pest and disease cycles and add nitrogen. A simple two-rotation plan — cereal, then legume — already improves yields compared to continuous rice or maize.

Test before you fertilise. A basic soil test tells you pH and nutrient levels so you buy inputs your field actually needs. Balanced application beats repeated blanket dosing, and it costs less over a season.
