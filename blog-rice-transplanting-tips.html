Rice Transplanting Tips for Better Yields

Small changes in nursery timing, seedling age and spacing add up to a noticeably better harvest.

Transplanting is where a rice crop is won or lost. Over-aged, stretched seedlings never recover, and a late nursery pushes the whole crop into a shorter, cooler grain-filling window.

Raise a healthy nursery. Use clean seed, a well-leveled bed and steady moisture. A nursery that dries out once can set the crop back by a week.

Transplant at the right seedling age. Young seedlings of 15 to 20 days establish faster than 30- to 40-day seedlings, which carry stored stress into the field.

Space deliberately. Regular spacing — roughly 20 by 20 centimetres — gives each hill room to tiller, improves air flow and makes weeding and harvesting easier.

Manage water carefully. Keep shallow water after transplanting while roots establish, then alternate wet and dry where your field allows. Continuous deep flooding wastes water and suppresses tillering.

Weed early and hand-weed once before tillering closes the canopy. Early weed removal protects yield far more than late, repeated weeding.

Common questions

Two to three seedlings per hill is a good general target for transplanting.

Count back from your ideal transplanting date and account for roughly three weeks of nursery growth.
