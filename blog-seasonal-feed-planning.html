Seasonal Feed Planning for Livestock

Feed is the biggest running cost on most livestock farms. Planning it by season keeps animals healthy and costs predictable.

Animal feed By Yagya · 26 Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Share

Feed planning starts with a calendar, not a sack price. Write down how many animals you will hold each month, what stage they are in — growing, milking, pregnant, resting — and match roughage and concentrate to that stage.

Build a roughage reserve before the dry months. Napier, grass hay, straw and fodder trees all lose quality if cut too late. Aim to store enough dry roughage to carry the herd through the months when fresh fodder is scarce.

Match concentrate to production stage. Grower, finisher and dairy rations are not interchangeable. Layer mash supports laying hens; starter crumble supports chicks. Feeding the wrong stage ration wastes money and underperforms.

Store feed properly. Keep sacks off the ground on pallets, in a dry, ventilated room, away from direct sun. Mouldy feed is a health risk as well as a loss — never feed visibly moulded material to animals.

Buy in planned lots rather than weekly. Bulk buying at the start of a season usually costs less and removes the risk of a local shortage. Keep a simple written record of stock, usage and cost per animal.

Common questions

Work out your monthly usage first, then hold two months of stock to ride out shortages and price swings.

Home mixing can save money if you can weigh ingredients accurately and balance the ration. Otherwise a formulated commercial ration is safer.
