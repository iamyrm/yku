Preventing Common Livestock Diseases

Most livestock losses come from a handful of preventable causes. Clean housing, clean water and a vaccination calendar cover most of it.

Prevention is cheaper than treatment in every livestock system. The losses that hurt small farms most — clostridial disease, respiratory infection, parasitism — are largely driven by housing, hygiene and contact management.

Keep housing clean and dry. Wet bedding and accumulated manure are the main source of infection and parasite load. Regular cleaning and good drainage do more for herd health than most medicines.

Isolate new arrivals. Keep purchased animals separate for at least two weeks, observe them, and only then introduce them to the main herd. Many outbreaks start with a single introduced animal.

Follow a vaccination calendar. Work out the locally recommended vaccinations for goats, cattle, pigs and poultry and put the dates on a wall calendar. Missing one dose is how preventable disease returns.

Watch for early signs. Reduced feed intake, isolation from the group, discharge from eyes or nose, limping or sudden drop in production are your first warning. Early isolation of a sick animal protects the rest of the herd.

Control parasites deliberately. Rotate pasture where possible, keep feeders off the ground, and plan deworming with a vet rather than dosing on a fixed blind schedule, which drives resistance.
